### Field System  

**The field system is the stationary part of a DC generator that produces the magnetic field in which the armature rotates.**  

It consists of:  

**Yoke – It is the outer frame of the machine. It supports the poles and provides a path for magnetic flux. This is usually made of solid cast steel. Pole pieces are attached to the inside of this circular frame.**  

***[Pole pieces are made by stacking many thin insulated steel sheets one on top of another. Each pole piece consists of two parts: the pole core and the pole shoe.]***  

**Pole cores – The central iron part of the pole pieces attached to the yoke.Field winding is wound around it. Carries magnetic flux from the field coil.**  

**Pole Shoe – The wider end part of the pole pieces attached to the pole core and facing the armature. Provides better support for the field winding. Pole shoes spread the magnetic flux uniformly across the air gap.**  

**Pole cores and Pole shoes are made of laminated steel sheets to reduce eddy current losses. become North and South poles when energized.**  

**Field windings – coils carrying DC current that create MMF and magnetic flux.**  

### Armature Core 

**It is a cylindrical laminated iron core mounted on the rotating shaft. It consists of many slotted soft-iron(a slice of slotted laminated iron) laminations. The slots to hold armature conductors(the copper wires). It rotates inside the magnetic field.**  

### Armature Windings 

**When conductors(copper wires) are placed between the slot of the armature core, it is called armature Windings. The armature Windings or bunch of copper wires can be inter-connected in series or parallel based on increasing current or voltage. To increase the voltage conductors are connected in series and to increase current conductors are connected in parallel.**  

### Commutator 

**A commutator is a mechanical rectifier that converts the alternating voltage generated in the armature winding into direct voltage across the brushes. It consists of copper segments insulated from each other, and the two ends of the armature coil (conductors) are connected to different commutator segments. In a simple DC generator, the commutator is divided into two segments. As the coil rotates in the magnetic field, the polarity of the induced EMF in the conductors reverses every half turn. At the same instant, the commutator segments interchange their contact with the fixed carbon brushes, thereby reversing the coil's connection to the external circuit. As a result, the polarity across the brushes remains the same, producing a unidirectional (DC) output voltage.**  

### Carbon Brushes 

**The carbon brushes ensures electrical connections between the rotating commutator and stationary external load. The brushes are made of carbon and rest on the commutator from the both side. The brush pressure is adjusted by means of adjustable springs. This is the main component which Carry current from the machine to the load. It works like a bridge between the machine and external load.**  


***[The mechanism of converting ac current to dc current by using commutator and brushes is like : when rotor/shaft rotates commutator also rotates with the rotor/shaft, the carbon brushes are fixed at their position. But after every polarity flips as the touch of commutator with carbon brush also flips automatically with the rotation, as a result the polarity remains same for the external load. Thus, the load only get current with same polarity.]***  

# A simple flow chart of the working Principle :  

Mechanical Energy  
       ↓  
Armature Rotation  
       ↓  
Flux Cutting  
       ↓  
Induced EMF  
       ↓  
Current in Armature Winding  
       ↓  
Commutator Action  
       ↓  
Brushes Collect Current  
       ↓  
DC Output
       ↓  
     Load  


***DC motor works in a same way as the DC generator but in reverse. DC motors convert electrical energy into mechanical energy. In this case, carbon brushes feed supply current to the commutator. After the brushes feed current to the commutator, the current flows through the armature winding. The current-carrying armature conductors interact with the magnetic field and experience a force. This force produces torque, causing the armature and shaft to rotate. Thus, the DC motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.***
